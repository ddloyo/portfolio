Data Catalog & Metric Definitions

A browsable inventory of a real dbt warehouse (87 models, 22 sources, 429 tests) and the dictionary that says, in business language, what each metric means and where it comes from.

What we found

The warehouse is well tested but thinly explained: 429 tests, yet only 3% of its columns have a description.

We catalogued 87 models and 22 sources with their lineage and test status, and defined 46 metrics in business language. Watch out: “sales” is 6 different figures depending on the report — the dictionary says which is which.

What the data says

  • 86 of 87 models have a description in dbt, but only 16 of 534 columns (3%). In gold, 0 of 116 columns are described — and those are exactly the ones the business sees.
  • 426 tests pass and 3 sit in warning on purpose, all on stg_erp__factura_linea: they are real findings of the source (broken references and totals that do not add up) set to warn so they get followed up without blocking the pipeline.
  • stg_crm__cliente is the most critical model: it feeds 10 of 13 dashboards (34 downstream models). A change there shows up across almost the whole portfolio.
  • With no tests at all: int_canal_mes_metricas, int_ventas_sucursal_pendiente. 8 of the 22 sources have none either — they are the ones that carry the mess on purpose.
  • “Sales” is not one number: there are 6 figures with that name (sales by team, executive revenue, monthly revenue, branch sales, invoiced amount…), each with a different scope. See the comparison table in the dictionary.
  • 3 of 6 scorecard KPIs are manually captured and their formula is not documented in the warehouse: confirm it with their owner before comparing them with a computed KPI.
