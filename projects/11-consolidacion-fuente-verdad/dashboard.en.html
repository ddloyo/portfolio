What the Data Shows

  • Q1 · The single source of truth prevents $18,111 MXN in inflated sales (26 duplicate rows across the 3 sources) against a consolidated total of $1,170,834 MXN — this settles the meeting debate over which number is correct.
  • Q2 · CDMX concentrates the greatest quality risk (exact duplicates, 4.0% of its rows) and accounts for 96% of all duplicate value detected — it is the source to fix first, at the point of origin.
  • Q3 · $12,018 MXN (estimated) remains unreported in the manual review queue — 12 rows, 100% from Monterrey — because the single source of truth never invents a missing amount; someone has to capture it.
  • Q4 · No branch declines steadily across the quarter: CDMX dropped in July and recovered in August, Monterrey followed the reverse pattern, and Guadalajara grew for two straight months despite being the smallest.
  • Q5 · The consolidated total smooths out each branch's swings: while CDMX and Monterrey oscillate ±19-28% month to month in opposite directions, the company total moved only -0.0% and +7.1% — planning off a single branch would have been misleading.
  • Q6 · A moderate weekly pattern exists: Monday sells 6% above the daily average and Saturday 11% below — use the seasonal index when setting daily targets, not a flat average.
  • Q7 · No anomalies were detected (>3 standard deviations from the 7-day rolling median) across the quarter's 92 days; daily variability (CV 32%) is normal sales noise, not an isolated event.
