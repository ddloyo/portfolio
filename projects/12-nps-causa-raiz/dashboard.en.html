What the data shows

  • Q1 · The drop isn't noise, it's a confirmed statistical anomaly: the 2026-08 NPS sits 4.1 standard deviations from its historical average (22.1) — well above the 3σ threshold that separates a real anomaly from normal month-to-month variation.
  • Q2 · The drop amounts to 27.6 NPS points (22.1 → -5.5), with the detractor rate nearly doubling (18.3% → 33.6%) and promoters falling from 40.4% to 28.1% — a real renewal risk in the newly dissatisfied base.
  • Q3 · This isn't broad-based deterioration, it's a concentrated cause: Response time went from 18.8% of the causes declared by detractors to 58.8% in the last 2 months — the fix needs to target the support process, not a generic 'improve customer experience' plan.
  • Q4 · The break is abrupt, not gradual: the first 10 months fluctuate with no clear trend (z between -1.2 and 1.6) and the drop happens all at once in 2026-07 — pointing to a specific operational change in support (staffing, tooling, SLA), not slow erosion.
  • Q5 · The drop is real, not a categorization artifact: the average score also fell from 7.97 to 7.33 in 2026-08 — consistent with a genuinely worse experience, not just responses crossing the line between 'passive' and 'detractor'.
  • Q6 · Response volume supports the signal: it stayed stable throughout the period (124-163 responses/month, 309 in the last 2 months) — the drop is not the product of a smaller or biased sample.
  • Q7 · Keep an eye on secondary causes even though they don't dominate today: Product quality (12.7%) and Customer service (10.8%) remain present among recent detractors — focusing effort on support shouldn't mean dropping monthly follow-up on the others.
  • Q8 · Recommended action: escalate this week, not at the next monthly cutoff: with the anomaly confirmed, the cause concentrated at 58.8%, and a solid sample volume, review support SLA and staffing for the last 2 months and set a recovery target (NPS ≥ 8.3) within 2 months, with biweekly follow-up for the duration of the correction.
